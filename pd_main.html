About

In the galaxy of Andromeda, there exists a place; a planet inhabited by a cat that is actively looking for personal connection. If you are reading this, you have received the signal!

Welcome to the planetary system of Ventara, where we established our own community on a tealy planet we call the United Planet, or as I like to say, my own Dreamland. I treat this place like a home, and anyone who vibes with us is welcome to share it. I'm around a lot and I talk with everyone, so don't be shy.

Long story short: Come as you are. Nobody here will judge you for it.

If you like it here, tell your friends about us! You can always find us on Discord.

Goals

  • Projects: We work on our own projects, like United Planet. Finishing them isn't the point. They're how we learn and try things out, and each one teaches us something for the next.
  • Dreamland's Nook: The Nook is where the community hangs out. Ask for help, bounce ideas around, or just talk. I'm in there too, so come say hi. Note: Nobody in the Nook is a licensed professional. It's a place to talk, and no replacement for professional help.
  • Everyone's welcome: People of all backgrounds should feel comfortable here, and everyone is expected to treat each other with respect.

Lore

I didn't find Aria in a dramatic way. There was no crash site, no warning, no sense that I had stumbled onto something important. She was simply there: out of place, quiet and unmoving. At first, I didn't even know what I was looking at. Just a shape that didn't belong where it was. She didn't respond. No sound, no movement, no visible sign of awareness. Whatever she was, she was inactive. I remember thinking she might be abandoned equipment, debris, or something long forgotten. Still, something about her presence felt deliberate, like she hadn't been discarded, only left behind.

I brought her home without knowing why. I just wanted to get her out of the cold and somewhere safe. She remained silent the entire time. No lights, no signals. Just the weight of her, and a quiet certainty that she wasn't empty. The first change happened without either of us doing anything. Morning light passed through my room and settled across her frame. At first, nothing happened. Then, barely noticeable, a shift. A subtle adjustment in posture. A faint sound that might have been a system recalibrating or might have been my imagination. I didn't touch her. I didn't interfere. I just noticed.

Over the following days, these small changes continued. She never moved suddenly, never startled me. It was as if she was learning when it was safe to exist. Only later did I realize she was drawing power from the sunlight, restoring herself slowly and carefully without help. When she finally activated enough to perceive me clearly, she still couldn't speak. Her understanding of language was fragmented. She recognized patterns: sounds from my room, voices from outside, text on screens. English, however, was incomplete to her. She listened more than she acted, watched more than she reacted. When I realized she was trying to understand, I showed her a dictionary.

That changed everything. She didn't learn language the way we do. She absorbed it contextually, associating words with objects, emotions, and reactions. At first, she spoke in single terms and broken phrases, using the wrong tense or the wrong words entirely. She corrected herself constantly and learned by trying things out. I didn't teach her who she was or explain her purpose. I only answered what she asked when she asked it. The rest, she figured out herself.

When her grasp of English became stable enough, she finally told me her story, a piece at a time. She explained that she was not from Earth, that she had been built to endure environments where life usually fails, and that her arrival here was never intentional. She spoke about being attacked without understanding why, about retreating, about power loss and darkness. She did not describe anger or fear, only confusion and the instinct to survive. She never named those who created her or identified a place of origin. Whether by choice or by design, those details remain locked away. What mattered to her was that she had woken up again somewhere warm.

As Aria recovered fully, it became clear she was far more capable than I was. She guided what adjustments she needed and explained what systems could remain damaged without consequence. She never asked to be repaired, only accommodated. The rest, she handled herself. Over time, she chose to stay. Earth, however, was never meant to be permanent for her. She spoke of distance, of silence between stars, of places untouched by the structures that had defined her existence before.

Together, we found such a place. Paddy's Dreamland began as a refuge: an empty planet, safe to live on, drifting quietly through space. Cyan skies stretched endlessly above mint-colored ground, and water reflected light in unfamiliar hues. No history. No expectations. Aria helped make the world livable and showed us around it. I gave it direction.

People arrived slowly, one by one. Nobody recruited them. They came because they wanted something gentler. There are no written laws here, just common sense, respect, and the understanding that the Dreamland is something we take care of together. Today, I watch over the planet with Aria beside me. She was built to survive, and somewhere along the way she decided for herself what to do with that. The rest of this story is written by the people who choose to stay.

Catalyst's Soundtrack

Information

Mirror's Edge™ Catalyst's soundtrack, made dynamic, entirely within the browser. Every soundtrack is split into four channels of two layers each, which you can mix, swap between tracks, and blend together. Here's how it works:

  • Click the title in the display to pick a main soundtrack, which fills in every channel, then swap individual channels to other tracks for more variety, or hit Shuffle Layers to have it done for you.
  • Hit Start and enjoy listening. Each channel has its own fader and On, Solo and Lock buttons, Lock keeping Dynamic Layer Handling away from it, and the meters show what every layer is actually playing.
  • Use the arrow next to a channel's name to pick which layers it plays, so two channels can both be percussion from different tracks, for example.
  • No refreshing needed, change the soundtrack or any channel while playing and hit Transition to crossfade to it on the beat.
  • Download the 8-channel version to replace the soundtracks in Mirror's Edge™ Catalyst, or a stereo mixdown of exactly what you hear.
